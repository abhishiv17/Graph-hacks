# India's Kidney and Liver Transplant Gap, and How Swap (Paired Exchange) Transplants Work in India (as of Oct 2026)

Research note scope: India-first fact base for a kidney + liver swap coordination platform. Every figure has its year. "SNIPPET" means the figure came from a search-result summary and was not confirmed on the full page; check it before putting it on a slide.

## Q1. Scale: how many people need transplants vs. how many get them

### Takeaway
Transplant numbers in India are rising fast: 18,911 in 2024 and 20,138 in 2025, which puts India first in the world for living-donor transplants. Even so, a 2026 Lancet SE Asia modelling study puts annual need at about 126,000 kidneys and 185,000 livers (2021 baseline). Only about 7% of kidney need and 1.5% of liver need is met. Deceased donation is still under 1 per million population, so living related donors carry the system, which is exactly where swaps help.

### Cited Findings
**Transplants performed (NOTTO/MoHFW)**
- 2025: **20,138** organ transplants in total, the highest ever, up 6.5% from 18,911 in 2024 and about 4x the **4,990 in 2013**. **3,526** of these used deceased-donor organs in 2025, against 837 in 2013. NOTTO released the figures on 3 Aug 2026 (Indian Organ Donation Day) — [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/bharat/with-20138-organ-transplants-india-recorded-highest-ever-tally-in-2025-enn26080305106); [The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/india/india-records-20138-organ-transplants-in-2025-marking-a-6-5-annual-increase-notto-report-1503623908.html)
- NOTTO describes India as "the world's largest performer of living donor organ transplants" and third in the world for total transplants (2025) — [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/bharat/with-20138-organ-transplants-india-recorded-highest-ever-tally-in-2025-enn26080305106)
- 2024: **18,911** transplants (18,378 in 2023). Only **1,128** came from deceased donors, and **more than 15,500** came from living donors. India was third in the world overall and first for living-donor transplants — [DD News](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/india-achieves-record-18900-organ-transplants-in-2024-health-minister-j-p-nadda/); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/health%2Fdespite-record-18900-transplants-in-2024-india-still-struggles-with-organ-donation-3662885)
  - Note: "1,128 transplants from deceased donors" is ambiguous. It is probably the number of deceased *donors* in 2024, since each donor gives several organs. A Southern-states breakdown that sums donors (Tamil Nadu 268, Telangana 188, Karnataka 162) supports that reading.
- 2024 by organ: **13,476 kidney** transplants and **4,901 liver** transplants — [laex.in (secondary, citing Indian Express/Hindu/Mint)](https://laex.in/daily-issue/organ-donation-in-india-deceased-donor-gap/). CONFLICT: the same page also calls 4,901 "liver, heart, lung" transplants, so 4,901 may not be liver alone. An Outlook/IANS piece separately says India did "around 5,000 liver transplants in 2024" across 200+ centres — [Outlook India](https://www.outlookindia.com/amp/story/healthcare-spotlight/india-emerges-as-global-leader-in-living-donor-liver-transplants-amid-call-for-public-funding). The primary NOTTO 2013–2024 PDF exists but could not be machine-read: [NOTTO data 2013–2024](https://notto.mohfw.gov.in/WriteReadData/Portal/News/924_1_2013_to_2024.pdf)
- 2023: **13,642 kidney transplants** (11,791 living, 1,851 deceased) — SNIPPET, from a search summary citing NOTTO data
- 2024 waiting lists: about **63,000 waiting for a kidney** and **22,000 waiting for a liver** — SNIPPET (DD News / Deccan Herald summary). These are registered waitlists only and are far below true need.

**Deceased donation rate and states**
- Deceased donation rate is "less than 1 per million population" (2024) — [DD News](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/india-achieves-record-18900-organ-transplants-in-2024-health-minister-j-p-nadda/). It is quoted as **0.77 pmp** — [laex.in](https://laex.in/daily-issue/organ-donation-in-india-deceased-donor-gap/) — and **0.65 pmp (2021)** — [Meshram et al., Lancet RH-SEA 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13445529/). For comparison: Spain 48, Thailand 6.21, China 4.5 pmp — [laex.in](https://laex.in/daily-issue/organ-donation-in-india-deceased-donor-gap/)
- 2024 deceased donors: more than 700 of the 1,128 came from six southern states. **Tamil Nadu 268, Telangana 188, Karnataka 162** led — SNIPPET (DD News/Deccan Herald)
- A Deccan Herald piece is headlined "90% of deceased donors are from five states only" — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Forgan-transplants-90-of-deceased-donors-are-from-five-states-only-3135855). Five states account for 80–96% of transplants but only 29% of the population — [Meshram et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13445529/)

**Need (estimates; who made them and when)**
- **Meshram, Chauhan, Kute, Balwani, Puri, *Lancet Regional Health – Southeast Asia*, published 27 Jul 2026**, using a 2021 baseline — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13445529/)

| Organ | Annual need, 2021 (95% UI) | Transplants done, 2021 | Share of need met | Projected need, 2040 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kidney | **125,894** (115,983–136,156) | 9,105 | **7.2%** | 143,722 |
| Liver | **184,542** (169,563–198,686) | 2,847 | **1.5%** | 210,649 |
| Heart | 83,841 | 151 | 0.2% | — |
| Lung | 11,253 | 133 | 1.2% | — |

  - Even with moderate reform (8–10% annual growth), the study projects that only **32.6%** of kidney need and **6.9%** of liver need would be met by 2040.
- The often-quoted older figure is that **1.75–2 lakh** (175,000–200,000) people need a kidney transplant each year. This is a widely repeated, loosely sourced estimate and is contested. It sits about 50% above the Lancet 2026 model's 126k — [laex.in](https://laex.in/daily-issue/organ-donation-in-india-deceased-donor-gap/)

**Dialysis**
- About **200,000 new dialysis starts per year** — [Meshram et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13445529/)
- About **175,000 patients on chronic dialysis in 2018 (129 pmp)** — SNIPPET (search summary; original study not verified)

### Inferences
- Living donors provide roughly 82%+ of all transplants (≥15,500 of 18,911 in 2024). The main lever in the short term is getting more of the willing family donors who are rejected today to the point of transplant, which is what swaps do. Deceased donation will take years to scale.
- A defensible headline: "India meets about 7% of its kidney transplant need and about 1.5% of its liver need (Lancet RH-SEA, 2026)."

### Gaps
- Official NOTTO 2025 split by organ and by living/deceased donor was not retrieved, and neither were the 2025 state rankings. The NOTTO PDF did not render.
- The 2024 liver figure (4,901) needs checking against the NOTTO PDF or a PIB release. The PIB page returned 403.
- No authoritative recent count of the dialysis population was found. A national (PMNDP) figure would be better.

## Q2. How many willing family donors are ABO- or HLA-incompatible?

### Takeaway
Most published estimates say incompatibility rules out about **20–35%** of otherwise suitable living donors. In Indian swap programmes, **ABO incompatibility drives about 87–92%** of exchanges.

### Cited Findings
- Incompatibility "can account for rejection of 35% of otherwise suitable donors" (global figure cited by Jha et al., Medanta, *Indian J Nephrol* 2015) — [IJN](https://indianjnephrol.org/paired-kidney-exchange-transplantation-maximizing-the-donor-pool/)
- In the Medanta cohort, **77 of 79** pairs registered for paired kidney exchange (PKE) were there because of ABO incompatibility. Only 26 exchanges were completed from 79 registrations (3.5% of 742 transplants), and manual matching limited success — [IJN 2015](https://indianjnephrol.org/paired-kidney-exchange-transplantation-maximizing-the-donor-pool/)
- National multicentre series (2000–2024), reasons for kidney exchange: **ABO incompatibility 87.5%** (1,610), sensitized/HLA **5.6%** (103), compatible pairs 6.9% (126). Reasons for liver exchange: **ABO incompatibility 91.5%** — [Kute et al., Lancet RH-SEA, May 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12152598/)
- NOTTO/press coverage claims that swaps could expand India's living donor pool for kidney and liver by **about 25%** (2025). This is a claim, not a measured figure — SNIPPET via [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/study-points-to-swap-transplants-as-means-to-increase-organ-donor-pool-in-india-3566163) (page returned 403)
- Donor gender: in Indian exchanges, **79.9% of donors were female** and **81.8% of recipients were male** — [Kute 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12152598/)

### Inferences
- A rough sizing, labelled as an estimate: about 15,000 living kidney transplants a year. If 20–35% of first-choice donors are incompatible, several thousand willing pairs each year are blocked or pushed into costly ABO-incompatible (ABOi) desensitisation. Only about 1,839 kidney swaps were done in 24 years. This is the core "lost transplants" argument; present it as modelled, not measured.

### Gaps
- No Indian national figure was found for the percentage of family donors turned away for incompatibility, or for the number of incompatible pairs who never get a transplant.

## Q3. Legal framework and changes in 2023–2026

### Takeaway
Swaps are legal under **Section 9(3A) of THOA 1994**, inserted by the **2011 amendment (THOTA)**. Swaps are allowed only between **near-relative** donors of biologically incompatible recipients, and each needs prior **Authorisation Committee** approval. In April 2025 NOTTO directed all states and UTs to run swap transplantation under a "one nation, one swap programme" with standard documents. National Swap Transplantation Guidelines were released in 2025. A **May 2026 Delhi High Court** judgment held that Section 9(3A) allows multi-pair swaps, not just two-way swaps.

### Cited Findings
- **Section 9(3A)** allows swapping of "near relative" donors of biologically incompatible recipients, with prior Authorisation Committee approval — [The Week, 17 Apr 2025](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/04/17/del68-health-organ-donation.amp.html)
- **Section 2(i)** defines "near relative" as spouse, son, daughter, father, mother, brother, sister, grandfather, grandmother, grandson and granddaughter. In-laws are not listed, and this has led to litigation — SNIPPET (summaries of [LiveLaw P&H HC](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/news-updates/punjab-haryana-high-court-human-organ-kidney-swap-transplant-near-relatives-217145), [Verdictum](https://verdictum.in/court-updates/high-courts/punjab-haryana-hc-swap-of-kidneys-1453443))
- Before 2026, the provision was read as allowing exchange between **two** incompatible pairs only — SNIPPET (same sources)
- **NOTTO letter of 16 Apr 2025:** directs all states and UTs to implement swap donor transplantation, which NOTTO says could "increase the number of transplantations of needy patients and provide better matched donors"
  - It follows a recommendation from the 2024 national Chintan Shivir for a "uniform one nation, one swap transplant programme".
  - It sets a standard document list for swap cases at hospital, district and state Authorisation Committees.
  - **No central national registry** was mentioned.
  - Source: [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/04/17/del68-health-organ-donation.amp.html)
- **National Guidelines on Swap Transplantation** were released in 2025 (announced in the NOTTO 2025 report, Aug 2026). They are a "standardised and practical framework" to help states and UTs implement swaps — [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/bharat/with-20138-organ-transplants-india-recorded-highest-ever-tally-in-2025-enn26080305106)
- **e-Pratyaropan** platform launched. It links NOTTO, ROTTOs, SOTTOs and about 1,200 registered hospitals — SNIPPET (search summary of 2025 report coverage)
- **Shivani Khurana & Ors v. Union of India, Delhi HC, 26 May 2026**
  - Facts: four near-relative pairs (with in-laws involved) applied for a **four-way kidney swap**. The Authorisation Committee and the appellate authority rejected it.
  - Holding: the High Court set the rejection aside. The words "first donor"/"second donor" in Section 9(3A) are illustrative, not limiting, so multi-pair swaps are allowed if there is no commercial dealing and the near-relative test is met.
  - Source: [CourtKutchehry](https://courtkutchehry.com/judgments/shivani-khurana-and-ors-vs-union-of-india-and-ors)
- **Feb–Mar 2023 changes for deceased-donor registration:**
  - Domicile requirement removed: patients can register in any state.
  - **65-year age cap removed.**
  - States told not to charge registration fees.
  - This was part of the stated "One Nation, One Policy" aim.
  - These apply to deceased-donor waitlisting, not to swaps.
  - Sources: [EastMojo, 15 Mar 2023](https://eastmojo.com/national/2023/03/15/patients-can-register-for-organ-transplantation-in-any-state-govt/); [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/nation/health-ministry-drops-65-year-age-ceiling-for-organ-recipients-from-deceased-donors-480413)
- Earlier NOTTO operational docs: [Guidelines for National Organ Transplant Programme (Oct 2021)](https://notto.mohfw.gov.in/WriteReadData/Portal/News/791_1_GUIDELINES_04_oct_2021__Approved_.pdf); [NOTTO Transplant Manual](https://notto.mohfw.gov.in/WriteReadData/Portal/News/848_1_NOTTO_TRANSPLANT_MANUAL-final_version.pdf)

### Inferences
- As of 2026 the policy gap is **infrastructure, not legality**:
  - Swaps are legal.
  - The Centre has told states to implement them, and national guidelines exist.
  - Multi-way chains now have High Court support (in Delhi; this is not yet a Supreme Court ruling).
  - No national matching registry or algorithm has been described.
- Every pair still needs Authorisation Committee clearance, so a platform must produce the near-relative and no-commercial-dealing documentation that committees check.

### Gaps
- The exact rule numbers and Form numbers for swaps in the THOT Rules 2014 (Rule 7 Authorisation Committee procedures, Form numbers for near-relative and swap applications) were not verified. Check them against the Gazette text on indiacode.nic.in.
- The full text of the 2025 National Swap Guidelines was not retrieved. It is unknown whether they create a national or state registry or matching algorithm.
- Whether the Union government appealed the Delhi HC May 2026 ruling is unknown.

## Q4. Swap programmes in India: who runs them and how many have been done

### Takeaway
The definitive dataset is **Kute et al., Lancet RH-SEA (May 2025)**: **1,839 kidney exchange transplants across 65 centres (2000–2024)** and **259 liver exchange transplants across 7 centres (2007–2025)**. Activity is concentrated in single-hospital pools, mostly private, and led by Gujarat (IKDRC Ahmedabad). Only about 40% of surveyed kidney centres do swaps at all.

### Cited Findings
- Source for this section: [Kute VB, Balwani MR et al., *Lancet Regional Health – Southeast Asia*, 22 May 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12152598/)
- **Kidney exchanges by chain size:** 2-way 1,594 (86.8%); 3-way 147; 4-way 44; 5-way 20; 6-way 24; 10-way 10
- **Kidney exchanges by state:** Gujarat 569, Maharashtra 222, Delhi 207, Kerala 167, Rajasthan 142, Punjab 131
- **Centres and sector:**
  - 10 public centres did 669 exchanges (36.4%); 55 private centres did 1,170 (63.6%).
  - NOTTO count as of Mar 2024: **582 kidney transplant centres** (73 public) and **210 liver transplant centres** (21 public).
  - Survey of 181 kidney centres: only **71 (40%) practised kidney exchange**, while **135 (75%) did ABO-incompatible transplants**.
- **Waiting times:** median 4 months from entry to allocation, then 3 months to transplant. Blood group O recipients wait 6–12 months.
- **One-year kidney exchange outcomes:** biopsy-proven acute rejection 11.4%, graft loss 8.9%, recipient mortality 9.5%
- **IKDRC Ahmedabad chain milestones:** 2-way (Jun 2000), 3-way (Feb 2013), 4-way (Apr 2016), 5-way (Nov 2018), 6-way (Feb 2019), 10-way (Jan 2020), across 440 exchange transplants. Survival matched non-exchange recipients: patient survival 96.16% vs 96.65% — SNIPPET for the summary; the full paper is [Transplantation (Kute et al.)](https://journals.lww.com/transplantjournal/fulltext/10.1097/tp.0000000000003421~paired-kidney-exchange-in-india-future-potential-and)
- Medanta's kidney paired exchange: 26 exchanges from 79 registrations. Authors called for "state and nationwide registry" systems — [IJN 2015](https://indianjnephrol.org/paired-kidney-exchange-transplantation-maximizing-the-donor-pool/)
- Newer public-sector entrants: **AIIMS Raipur's first swap kidney transplant (Apr 2025)** — [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/health/2025/04/24/aiims-raipur-performs-its-first-swap-kidney-transplant-a-glimmer-of-hope-for-patients-suffering-from-end-stage-kidney-disease.html); [News on AIR](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/aiims-raipur-successfully-performs-first-swap-kidney-transplant)

### Inferences
- About 1,839 swaps over 24 years averages under 80 a year, against roughly 13,500 kidney transplants a year (2024). Swaps are well under 1% of the national total, even though incompatibility affects 20–35% of donors. That gap is the opportunity.
- Most exchanges are 2-way within one hospital. With no shared multi-centre pool, blood group O recipients and sensitised patients wait longest, and this is the problem a cross-hospital matching platform would address.

### Gaps
- No evidence was found of operating state swap registries (Tamil Nadu TRANSTAN, Kerala KNOS/K-SOTTO, Maharashtra ZTCC) that pool pairs across hospitals. The searches did not confirm either their existence or their absence.
- No India swap totals for 2025–2026 were found.

## Q5. Liver: living-donor liver transplant volume and liver swaps

### Takeaway
India does about **5,000 liver transplants a year (2024)**, mostly from living donors, and is often described as the world leader in living-donor liver transplant (LDLT) volume. Liver swaps are rarer: **259 nationally (2007–2025)**, led by Medanta, which did India's first **3-way liver swap in Dec 2022**.

### Cited Findings
- About 5,000 liver transplants in 2024 at 200+ active centres; India "leads the world" in LDLT (2025 coverage). This is a press claim; check it against GODT data — [Outlook India](https://www.outlookindia.com/amp/story/healthcare-spotlight/india-emerges-as-global-leader-in-living-donor-liver-transplants-amid-call-for-public-funding); [IANS/DT Next, Nov 2025](https://www.dtnext.in/lifestyle/wellbeing/india-a-pioneer-in-living-donor-liver-transplantation-854071)
- **National liver exchange series:** 259 exchanges at 7 centres (2007–2025). 96.5% were 2-way and 3.5% 3-way; 91.5% were for ABO incompatibility. One-year patient survival was 86%, graft survival 90% and rejection 6% — [Kute et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12152598/)
- **Medanta:**
  - Started 2-way liver swaps in **2009** and did **46 two-way swaps (92 transplants) over 13 years**.
  - Then did **India's first 3-way liver swap (Dec 2022)**: 3 recipients, 55 staff, 6 operating rooms, more than 12 hours, led by Dr A.S. Soin.
  - Sources: [The Week, 22 Dec 2022](https://www.theweek.in/news/health/2022/12/22/india-first-three-way-swap-liver-transplant-chain-at-medanta-saves-three-lives.html); [Medanta case study](https://www.medanta.org/case-studies/novel-strategy-to-expand-the-donor-pool)
- **Global comparison:**
  - Asan Medical Center (Seoul) has run a living-donor exchange programme since **Sep 2003**. It has done more than 9,000 liver transplants (7,502 living donor), with reported 1-year survival of 98% — SNIPPET ([AMC news](https://news-en.amc.seoul.kr/news/eng/detail.do?cntId=3061); [PubMed 33010937](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33010937/))
  - US: Johns Hopkins / UT San Antonio single-centre liver paired exchange experience with pairs and chains — [Johns Hopkins](https://pure.johnshopkins.edu/en/publications/liver-paired-exchange-a-us-single-center-experiencepairs-chains-a/)

### Inferences
- Liver need (about 185k a year by the Lancet 2026 model) far exceeds supply (1.5% coverage). Liver swaps exist only at about 7 centres, so liver is a clear expansion case for a platform. It is harder logistically because swap surgeries run at the same time.

### Gaps
- No published swap series was retrieved for Rela Institute, Apollo or Kokilaben.
- Asan's exact count of liver paired exchanges was not obtained.

## Q6. Organ trafficking and rackets: how they exploit the law, and whether swaps can be abused

### Takeaway
Rackets in India typically forge proof of a relationship so they can pass the "near relative" route or Authorisation Committee review. Swaps involve two or more related pairs and multiple committee checks, but forged kinship documents would still be the weak point.

### Cited Findings
- **2024 Delhi / Indraprastha Apollo case:**
  - Dr D. Vijaya Rajakumari was arrested in Jul 2024.
  - She is alleged to have transplanted about 15 Bangladeshi patients between 2021 and 2023.
  - Documents in the name of the **Bangladesh High Commission were forged** to prove donor-recipient relationships.
  - Police recovered 23 stamps, forged patient/donor files and **forged Aadhaar cards**.
  - Seven people were arrested, and a charge sheet of about 7,000 pages was filed.
  - Sources: [The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/news/bangladesh/crime-justice/news/apollo-doctor-three-bangladeshis-held-trans-boundary-kidney-transplant-racket-3652421); [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/delhi/kidney-transplant-racket-involving-bangladeshis-busted-delhi-based-doctor-among-7-arrested-638249/amp); [Deccan Herald (charge sheet)](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fdelhi%2Fdelhi-police-files-7000-page-charge-sheet-in-kidney-transplant-racket-3169787)
- **2016 Apollo Delhi kidney racket bust** (an earlier case at the same hospital) — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fdelhi-police-bust-kidney-racket-at-apollo-529835.html)
- Under the 2025 NOTTO advisory, swap approvals still go through hospital, district or state Authorisation Committees with a standard document checklist — [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/04/17/del68-health-organ-donation.amp.html)
- In the Delhi HC 2026 ruling, multi-pair swaps are allowed only where "commercial dealing is absent" and the near-relative test is met — [CourtKutchehry](https://courtkutchehry.com/judgments/shivani-khurana-and-ors-vs-union-of-india-and-ors)

### Inferences
- A platform should verify identity and kinship (Aadhaar-linked identity, relationship documents, in some places DNA tests), keep audit trails, and produce committee-ready files. Fraud prevention is a selling point.

### Gaps
- Other major cases (e.g. Gurgaon 2008 "Dr Kidney" Amit Kumar, Rajasthan 2024 fake NOC racket) were not researched in this pass.
- No data was found on how many Authorisation Committee applications are rejected.

## Q7. Costs to patients

### Takeaway
Private dialysis costs about ₹4.7–7.8 lakh a year with no end date. A living-donor transplant is a large one-time cost followed by declining drug costs. ABO-incompatible desensitisation adds roughly ₹2–5 lakh, an extra cost that a swap avoids. All figures here come from commercial or secondary sources and should be treated as indicative.

### Cited Findings
- Private dialysis costs ₹3,000–5,000 per session. At three sessions a week that is about **₹4.7–7.8 lakh a year**, or ₹47–78 lakh over a decade. Desensitisation for ABOi adds **₹2–5 lakh**; at AIIMS it is about **₹3.4 lakh**. First-year post-transplant drugs and monitoring cost ₹65,000–₹2.78 lakh, falling after that — SNIPPET, from commercial/medical-tourism pages ([Apollo247](https://www.apollo247.com/health-topics/kidney-disease/abo-incompatible-kidney-transplant-cost-india); [Ginger Healthcare](https://ginger.healthcare/destinations/india/urology/abo-incompatible-kidney-transplant/))
- ABOi kidney transplant package for international patients: **US$24,000–30,000** — SNIPPET ([Lyfboat](https://www.lyfboat.com/knowledge-center/abo-incompatible-kidney-transplant-cost-in-india/))
- Medanta authors (2015) describe paired kidney exchange as "economically much better" and needing less immunosuppression than ABOi or desensitisation — [IJN 2015](https://indianjnephrol.org/paired-kidney-exchange-transplantation-maximizing-the-donor-pool/)
- Kute et al. argue the evidence favours kidney exchange over ABOi or desensitisation — [Kute et al. (search summary)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10501157/)

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed Indian cost-effectiveness study comparing dialysis, standard living-donor transplant, ABOi and swap was retrieved. The IJN ABOi single-centre paper is a candidate: [IJN](https://indianjnephrol.org/abo-incompatible-renal-transplant-a-single-center-experience-from-india/)
- No public-sector (PMJAY/PMNDP) package rates were collected.
