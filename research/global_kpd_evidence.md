# Global evidence on kidney paired donation (KPD): what works, measured results, where matches fail

Scope note: researched October 2026. Every figure carries its year. Items marked "(background, not re-verified)" come from well-known literature that I could not re-fetch in this session. Treat them as leads to check, not as citable facts.

## 1. Major programmes and their measured outcomes

### Takeaway
National and multi-centre KPD programmes now produce from tens to about 1,700 transplants a year each. The large US private registry (NKR) dominates. National public schemes (UK, Canada, Australia/NZ, Spain, Netherlands) typically transplant about 40-50% of the patients they enrol. Many proposed matches never reach surgery: the US OPTN pilot converts only about 10% of match offers into transplants.

### Cited Findings
**United States**
- US kidney exchange enabled roughly 800 transplants per year in the 2010-2014 study period. — [Agarwal et al., AER 2019](https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/aer.20180771)
- NKR handled 33.1% of all US kidney-exchange transplants in 2014, more than 5 times as many as APD (Alliance for Paired Donation, now APKD) and UNOS combined. — [Agarwal et al., AER 2019 (NSF PAR full text)](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10188997)
- NKR facilitated 1,744 transplants in 2024 and passed its 10,000th transplant. It is projected to handle about 30% of all US living-donor kidney transplants in 2025. The source is Wikipedia, citing NKR announcements, so it is secondary. — [Wikipedia: National Kidney Registry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Kidney_Registry)
- NKR data from 2010-2014: most chains involve 4-5 transplants, and chains (not cycles) now account for about 90% of NKR transplants. — [Agarwal et al., AER 2019](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10188997)
- OPTN KPD Pilot Program (UNOS), 1 Oct 2010 to 31 Dec 2020: 366 transplants in total. 124 of 215 kidney programmes (58%) entered at least one pair. On average 249 candidates were added each year. Over 4,000 match offers were sent. About 10% of matches have led to a transplant since 2016. 50% of identified matches were 3-way exchanges. 2-year graft survival was 93.4% and patient survival 96.4%. — [ATC abstract, "A Decennial Summary of the OPTN KPD Pilot Program"](https://atcmeetingabstracts.com/?p=30028)
- In 2013-2014 only 8% of OPTN KPDPP matches resulted in a transplant. — [ATC abstract on match offer refusals](https://atcmeetingabstracts.com/abstract/reasons-for-match-offer-refusals-and-efforts-to-reduce-them-in-the-optnunos-kidney-paired-donation-pilot-program-kpdpp/)

**United Kingdom (UKLKSS, NHSBT)**
- The scheme started in April 2007. It holds about 300 recipients registered at any one time. Matching runs are quarterly (January, April, July and October) and find 2-way and 3-way exchanges plus altruistic donor chains. — [ODT/NHSBT UKLKSS page](https://odt.nhs.uk/living-donation/uk-living-kidney-sharing-scheme); [Organ Donation Scotland](https://organdonationscotland.org/living-donation/uk-living-kidney-sharing-scheme)
- In 2024/25 there were 125 kidney transplants through the UKLKSS, and 60 people donated non-directed (altruistically). — [NHSBT data reported in press, Nov 2025](https://www.shropshirestar.com/showbiz/2025/11/04/altruistic-kidney-donation-in-spotlight-as-jesse-eisenberg-helps-stranger); primary: [NHSBT Annual Report 2024-25](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/nhs-blood-and-transplant-annual-report-and-accounts-2024-to-2025)
- 1 Aug 2013 to 31 Dec 2020: the Glasgow/NHSBT optimisation software identified 1,652 transplants, and 1,031 were performed. The UKLKSS accounts for about 20% of UK living-donor kidney transplant activity. Long altruistic chains make up about 25% of scheme activity and gave 273 extra transplants. — [University of Glasgow impact case study](https://www.gla.ac.uk/schools/computing/research/researchimpact/livingkidneytransplantation)
- The UK reported 658 cumulative KPD transplants as of around 2016-2017, with about 250 pairs per matching run. — [Biró et al., Transplantation 2019 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6613834/)

**Netherlands**
- The national programme started in 2004 and allows exchanges of up to 4 pairs. It uses a virtual crossmatch before the lab crossmatch. 284 transplants by 2016. — [Biró et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6613834/)
- Early Dutch results: 276 couples enrolled and 183 computer-matched combinations. 62 of the 183 (34%) had an unpredicted positive crossmatch. Overall transplant rate was about 40%, with excellent 5-year graft survival. — [Erasmus repository summaries](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/28954); [PubMed 17315491](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17315491/)

**Canada (Canadian Blood Services KPD)**
- Launched nationally in 2009. The 1,000th transplant was reached in May 2023. 51% of candidates who entered at least one match cycle were transplanted. Each match cycle yields 27 transplants on average, and chains finish about 4 months after they are proposed. 1-year outcomes: patient survival 100%, graft survival 98%, biopsy-proven acute rejection 8%. 73 transplants in 2018, the best year to that date. — [Canadian Blood Services 1,000 milestone](https://sang.ca/en/about-us/media/newsroom/1000-lifesaving-connections-canadian-blood-services-kidney-paired-donation); [CBS KPD professional page](https://professionaleducation.blood.ca/en/node/186); [UHN news](https://www.uhn.ca/corporate/News/Pages/Celebrating_Canada_1000th_kidney_transplant_thanks_to_the_Kidney_Paired_Donation_program.aspx)

**Australia and New Zealand (ANZKX)**
- Began in Australia in 2010-2011 and merged with the New Zealand programme in 2019, a first for binational exchange. Reached 500 transplants in 2022. Most matches are made within 6-12 months of a pair entering. Outcomes are equivalent to directed living donation. — [DonateLife ANZKX](https://www.donatelife.gov.au/ANZKX); [ANZKX 500 transplants news (2022)](https://www.donatelife.gov.au/news-events/news/2022/anzkx-reaching-500-kidney-transplants); [Information for patients](https://www.donatelife.gov.au/ANZKX/information-patients)

**Spain (ONT)**
- First cross-transplant in July 2009. 255 patients had benefited at the date of the press note, which is undated in the search snippet (likely around 2019). Matching runs every 4 months. 25 hospitals in 12 regions take part. Organs travel by high-speed train under an ONT-Renfe agreement. 18 altruistic donors enabled 55 transplants since 2010. — [Spanish Ministry of Health / ONT notes](https://www.sanidad.gob.es/gabinete/notasPrensa.do?id=5265); [ONT altruistic chains note](https://www.sanidad.gob.es/eu/gabinete/notasPrensa.do?id=4948)
- Biró 2019 reported 147 Spanish KPD transplants and about 110 pairs per run. Spain was cooperating with Italy and Portugal. — [Biró et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6613834/)

**South Korea**
- Korea ran a single-centre donor exchange ("swap") programme from 1991 without computerised matching. A web-based computerised algorithm was later tested across multiple centres, with excellent long-term outcomes reported. — [J Korean Med Assoc (KoreaMed Synapse)](https://synapse.koreamed.org/articles/1042059)

**Europe overall**
- Through end-2016, about 10 operating European KEPs (17 countries reporting) had done over 1,300 KPD transplants. That was about 8% of those countries' living kidney donations in 2015. — [Biró et al., "Building kidney exchange programmes in Europe", Transplantation 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6613834/)

### Inferences
- Large programmes produce one to two orders of magnitude more transplants than single-country start-up programmes. NKR has about 1,700 a year, while Austria, Belgium and Portugal had about 10 cumulative each by 2016. Scale and long chains are the main differentiators.
- A realistic "match rate" benchmark for a national pool is about 40-51% of enrolled patients eventually transplanted (Canada 51%, Netherlands about 40%, STEP 40%).

### Gaps
- I found no official 2025/2026 cumulative totals for APKD, UKLKSS (a 2,000th UK KPD transplant in September 2024 appeared in a search summary but I could not confirm the primary source), ANZKX, Spain or Korea.
- Median time to transplant by programme was not found, except Canada's chains finishing about 4 months after proposal and ANZKX's "most matches in 6-12 months".
- APKD-specific published totals were not retrieved.

## 2. Evidence that larger, shared (multi-centre, national, international) pools produce more transplants

### Takeaway
The strongest quantitative evidence is Agarwal et al. (AER 2019). 62% of US exchange transplants were arranged within single hospitals. Those hospitals operate far below efficient scale. Fixing the fragmentation would add about 240-500 transplants a year, 25-63% more. International pooling in Scandinavia (STEP) and the UK's move to national optimisation also show measurable gains.

### Cited Findings
- 62% of US kidney-exchange transplants involved a patient and donor from the same hospital. Only 41.4% of hospitals took part in NKR. Within participating hospitals, only 59.1% of exchange transplants went through NKR. — [Agarwal et al., AER 2019](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10188997)
- Production function estimates: NKR averaged 0.51 transplants per submitted donor. A platform half NKR's size would average 0.46, and one double the size 0.547. The median hospital has about 9 donor arrivals a year, giving 0.16 transplants per donor. The 90th percentile hospital (27 arrivals) gives 0.29. The largest single hospital (Methodist San Antonio, 109 arrivals) gives 0.42. UNOS and APD each give about 0.40. — [Agarwal et al. 2019](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10188997)
- An estimated 500.1 transplants a year are lost to fragmentation, against about 800 performed. The conservative lower bound is 143.7-243.5. The paper's headline is 240-500 extra transplants a year (25-63%), worth $240-500 million a year. The market can support 2-3 national platforms near efficient scale. — [Agarwal et al. 2019](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10188997)
- Single hospitals match inefficiently. In within-hospital exchanges outside the big platforms, 22.8% of blood group O donors go to non-O patients, compared with 6.5% at NKR. Mean PRA (panel reactive antibody) of patients transplanted within hospitals was 18, about half that at national platforms (about 35). About 1 in 6 NKR recipients have PRA above 90. — [Agarwal et al. 2019](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10188997)
- Segev et al. (JAMA, 20 April 2005) simulated a national KPD with optimised (maximum-weight) matching. It found more transplants, better HLA matching and more 5-year graft survival than a first-accept approach. Savings were $290 million over dialysis for any KPD, plus a further $50 million from optimised matching. Up to $750 million could be saved if 7% of waitlisted patients took part. — [ScienceDaily summary of JAMA 2005](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2005/06/050608054631.htm); [EurekAlert](https://eurekalert.org/news-releases/627035)
- UK: moving to the improved national optimisation algorithm (2013-2020) identified 1,652 transplants, compared with an estimated 1,146 under the 2007 algorithm. 1,031 transplants were performed, compared with an estimated 715. That is +316 transplants (+44%), with an estimated £76.2 million in NHS savings for 2013-2030. — [University of Glasgow impact case study](https://www.gla.ac.uk/schools/computing/research/researchimpact/livingkidneytransplantation)
- Scandiatransplant STEP (international, launched 2019), 2019-2022: 11 match runs plus 4 reruns, 114 pairs and 6 anonymous donors, about 22 pairs per run. 49 transplants (40% of enrolled pairs), 2-year graft survival 95.8%. 41% of transplants used kidneys imported from another Scandiatransplant country. Waiting recipients had median cPRA 98% and transplanted recipients 68%. — [Transplantation Direct 2023 (PMC10581625)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10581625)
- ANZKX became the world's first binational merger (Australia and NZ, 2019). Austria and the Czech Republic did their first transnational exchange in September 2016. Spain cooperates with Italy and Portugal. — [DonateLife](https://www.donatelife.gov.au/ANZKX); [Biró 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6613834/)

### Inferences
- For a hackathon pitch, the AER result is the strongest single citation. Pooling the same patients across hospitals into one optimised platform roughly triples the yield per donor (0.16 to 0.51) for a typical hospital. A median-size hospital matching alone wastes most of its incompatible pairs.
- The 41% cross-border share in STEP shows that a large share of transplants in a shared pool would not exist without the sharing.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the specific Roth/Sönmez/Ünver simulation numbers in this session. (Background, not re-verified: their 2007 AER paper "Efficient Kidney Exchange: Coincidence of Wants" shows that adding 3-way exchanges gives substantial gains over 2-way only, and that 4-way and longer add little in large pools.)
- No retrieved quantitative ENCKEP (COST CA15210) simulation of the gain from European pooling. ENCKEP papers by Biró, Klimentova, Viana and others exist; check them directly.

## 3. Cycles vs chains (and dominos)

### Takeaway
Cycles are capped at 2-3 pairs, at most 4 in the Netherlands, because all surgeries in a cycle must run at once so that no donor gives without their pair receiving. Chains started by non-directed (altruistic) donors can be non-simultaneous and long. They now dominate US practice (about 90% of NKR transplants) and are about 25% of UK activity. Simulation evidence shows chains give more transplants, especially for sensitised and blood group O patients.

### Cited Findings
- Cycles are carried out simultaneously and are usually limited to at most 3 pairs for logistical reasons. Chains can be long because the transplants need not be simultaneous. Most NKR chains have 4-5 transplants, and chains are about 90% of NKR transplants. — [Agarwal et al. 2019](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10188997)
- Rees et al. (NEJM 2009; 360:1096-1101) reported the first non-simultaneous extended altruistic-donor (NEAD) chain: 10 transplants started by one altruistic donor in July 2007, across 6 centres in 5 states, over 8 months. — [Rees et al. NEJM 2009 (PDF)](https://stanford.edu/%7Ealroth/papers/Rees%20etal%20NEJM%202009.pdf)
- Ashlagi, Gilchrist, Roth and Rees (AJT 2011; 11:984-994) simulated chains. When segments of 4-6 transplants are allowed, NEAD chains produce more transplants than simultaneous domino paired donation (DPD). NEAD chains also produce more transplants for highly sensitised and blood group O recipients. — [AJT 2011 PDF](https://stanford.edu/%7Ealroth/papers/Nonsimultaneous%20Chains%20AJT%202011.pdf); [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/64805)
- UK: long altruistic donor chains gave 273 extra transplants by December 2020 and are about 25% of scheme activity. 1,420 of the transplants identified in 2013-2020 came from 3-way exchanges and altruistic chains. — [University of Glasgow](https://www.gla.ac.uk/schools/computing/research/researchimpact/livingkidneytransplantation)
- Spain: 18 altruistic donors enabled 55 transplants (about 3 per donor) since 2010. — [ONT/Ministry note](https://www.sanidad.gob.es/eu/gabinete/notasPrensa.do?id=4948)
- Italy (from 2015) runs long but almost-simultaneous altruistic chains involving up to 11 centres. The Czech Republic has done 6- and 7-way exchanges with non-simultaneous surgeries allowed. France allows only 2-way exchanges and no altruistic donation. — [Biró 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6613834/)
- Agarwal et al. note that platforms commonly reward a hospital that submits an altruistic donor by matching one of its unpaired patients, which is the end of the chain. — [Agarwal 2019](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10188997)

### Inferences
- Domino paired donation means a chain that ends by giving the last donor's kidney to the deceased-donor waitlist. It is a natural chain-termination rule for a hackathon matcher.
- In practice, a matcher should support 2-cycles, 3-cycles and chains with a configurable maximum length. Common settings are cycle length 3, and chain length 4-6 or unbounded in the US.

### Gaps
- I found no precise national figures on how often non-simultaneous chains "break" because a bridge donor reneges. Published NKR/APKD reneging rates are reported as low, but I did not retrieve them here.

## 4. Failure of proposed matches, and failure-aware matching

### Takeaway
Most proposed matches fail before surgery. About 90% of OPTN pilot match offers do not lead to a transplant. About 38% of UK-identified transplants were not performed. 62.5% of STEP cycles and chains did not proceed, and 34% of early Dutch matches had an unexpected positive crossmatch. Failure-aware matching (Dickerson, Procaccia and Sandholm) maximises expected rather than nominal transplants. INFORMS reports it can roughly double successful transplants in some settings. Fallback options (re-runs, 3-cycles with back-arcs, short chains) partly recover broken matches.

### Cited Findings
- OPTN KPDPP: about 8% of matches led to transplant in 2013-2014, and about 10% since 2016. — [ATC abstract](https://atcmeetingabstracts.com/abstract/reasons-for-match-offer-refusals-and-efforts-to-reduce-them-in-the-optnunos-kidney-paired-donation-pilot-program-kpdpp/); [Decennial summary](https://atcmeetingabstracts.com/?p=30028)
- Failed OPTN match offers, Jan-Sept 2014: 52% (n=172) were accepted by the candidate's hospital but failed because another match in the same exchange was refused. This is the cascade effect. Of outright refusals, 32.6% were crossmatch-related, often antibodies to DQB or DPB that the screen did not capture. 34.4% were donor concerns (age, medical history). 21.9% were candidate issues, notably competing offers from other KPD programmes. — [ATC abstract](https://atcmeetingabstracts.com/abstract/reasons-for-match-offer-refusals-and-efforts-to-reduce-them-in-the-optnunos-kidney-paired-donation-pilot-program-kpdpp/)
- UK 2013-2020: 1,652 transplants identified and 1,031 performed, so about 62% proceeded and about 38% fell through. This is derived from the Glasgow figures. — [University of Glasgow](https://www.gla.ac.uk/schools/computing/research/researchimpact/livingkidneytransplantation)
- STEP 2019-2022: 35 of 56 identified cycles/chains (62.5%) did not proceed. Reasons:
  - 21 immunological: 16 unacceptable donor-specific antibodies, 4 high ABO titres, 1 positive crossmatch.
  - 11 other: withdrawal, clinical change or illness.
  - 3 administrative errors.

  Success by structure was 2-way 57%, 3-way 20% and chains 44%. — [Transplantation Direct 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10581625)
- Netherlands early phase: 62 of 183 computer-matched combinations (34%) had a positive crossmatch not predicted by screening. Alternatives were found for 39 couples. Clinical problems stopped the exchange for 51 couples, and alternatives were found for only 19. — [Erasmus/PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17315491/); [repub.eur.nl](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/28954)
- Dickerson, Procaccia and Sandholm, "Failure-Aware Kidney Exchange", Management Science 65(4):1768-1791 (2019; EC 2013 conference version):
  - Models a failure probability for each edge, so a cycle or chain succeeds only if all of its edges succeed.
  - When one match in a chain fails, all later transplants in that chain fail too.
  - Maximising expected transplants raises realised transplants in theory, in random graph models, on real UNOS match-run data 2010-2014, and in dynamic simulations.
  - INFORMS says the approach can "roughly double" successful transplants, depending on setting.

  — [INFORMS news release](https://www.informs.org/News-Room/INFORMS-Releases/News-Releases/Even-with-the-latest-innovations-in-kidney-exchange-most-planned-transplants-cannot-actually-be-carried-out-A-new-study-introduces-an-approach-that-can-roughly-double-success-rates); [SUFE summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/391958)
- The Czech programme explicitly builds in flexibility to re-match after positive crossmatch results. Several European KEPs run "reruns" (STEP did 4 reruns across 11 runs). — [Biró 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6613834/); [STEP 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10581625)

### Inferences
- STEP's per-structure success (2-way 57% > chains 44% > 3-way 20%) supports the failure-aware logic. Longer structures with all-or-nothing success fail more, so a matcher should weight by success probability, not just count.
- Back-arcs (background, not re-verified): the UK algorithm gives preference to 3-cycles that contain an embedded 2-cycle (a "back-arc"). If one pair drops out, a 2-way exchange can still go ahead. This is a standard UKLKSS objective in Manlove & O'Malley's work. Cite Manlove & O'Malley, ACM JEA 2015 "Paired and altruistic kidney donation in the UK: algorithms and experimentation" after checking.
- The 52% "cascade" failure share in the OPTN data is a direct quantitative argument for failure-aware and fallback-aware matching.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the exact headline percentage gains from the Management Science paper itself. INFORMS's "roughly double" is a press characterisation.
- I did not retrieve Bray et al. (failure rates in UNOS/APD data) or recent NKR failure rates.

## 5. Virtual crossmatch (VXM)

### Takeaway
A virtual crossmatch compares the recipient's HLA antibody profile (single-antigen bead, SAB, assay, with unacceptable antigens listed above an MFI threshold) with the donor's HLA typing. It predicts the physical crossmatch without mixing cells. It has very high sensitivity (about 100%) and good negative agreement (90-97%), but lower specificity (74-79%). Many residual failures involve DQA/DP antibodies that are poorly typed or poorly covered.

### Cited Findings
- VXM assesses immunological compatibility from two independent lab tests: patient anti-HLA antibodies and donor HLA typing. — [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10358445)
- In one study, negative VXM correlated with actual CDC crossmatch in 97% of cases and flow crossmatch in 90%. Sensitivity was 100%. Specificity was 74% (CDC-XM) and 79% (FC-XM), lowered by "acceptable" and anti-DQA/DPB donor-specific antibodies. — [Search-summarised from PubMed 25242749 and related reviews](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25242749)
- SAB limitations: limited coverage of common alleles, shared-epitope antibodies diluted across beads, and preferential binding to some beads. — [PMC8717962](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8717962)
- European KEPs commonly use a VXM and then confirm with a lab crossmatch. HLA unacceptability thresholds were typically 2000-3000 MFI (mean fluorescence intensity) in 2016-2017. — [Biró 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6613834/)
- Crossmatch-related refusals in OPTN KPD (32.6% of refusals) often involved DQB/DPB antibodies. — [ATC abstract](https://atcmeetingabstracts.com/abstract/reasons-for-match-offer-refusals-and-efforts-to-reduce-them-in-the-optnunos-kidney-paired-donation-pilot-program-kpdpp/)
- After the Dutch programme introduced better screening and VXM, unexpected positive crossmatches fell from the early 34%. This is inferred from the programme's evolution and is not a directly quantified figure. — see Gaps

### Inferences
- In the matcher, the compatibility edge test should be: ABO compatible AND no donor HLA antigen (including DQA1/DPA1/DPB1 when typed) in the recipient's unacceptable-antigen list above the MFI threshold. The edge failure probability should rise with the recipient's cPRA and with incomplete donor typing.

### Gaps
- I found no published Dutch, UK or NKR time series of positive-crossmatch rates before and after VXM.

## 6. Disadvantaged patients: highly sensitised and blood group O

### Takeaway
Pools fill up with hard-to-match patients: blood group O recipients and those with high cPRA. National programmes give these patients priority points and use O donors sparingly. Single hospitals instead use O donors for easy patients, which hurts these groups.

### Cited Findings
- NKR pool (2010-2014): 58.6% of patients in pairs were blood group O, compared with only 31.9% of their paired donors. Mean PRA was 47.6. NKR can match only about 50% of its donors. — [Agarwal 2019](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10188997)
- About half of NKR's apparently "inefficient" O-to-non-O transplants went to highly sensitised patients. Within-hospital O-to-non-O transplants almost never did. — [Agarwal 2019](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10188997)
- STEP: patients still waiting had median cPRA 98%, compared with 68% among those transplanted. International pooling raised matching probability for highly sensitised patients. — [STEP 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10581625)
- European KEP objectives typically maximise transplants and then weight HLA match, blood group, age difference, travel distance, sensitisation and waiting time. The UK considers sensitisation and waiting time and uses donor-donor age difference as a tie-breaker. Spain prioritises paediatric recipients. — [Biró 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6613834/)
- In UK deceased-donor allocation, "highly sensitised" means calculated reaction frequency (cRF) of 85% or more. This is a UK convention analogous to cPRA. — [Mumford et al., Transpl Int 2019](https://frontierspartnerships.org/journals/transplant-international/articles/10.1111/tri.13418/full)
- NEAD chains produce more transplants for highly sensitised and O recipients than domino chains. — [Ashlagi et al. AJT 2011](https://stanford.edu/%7Ealroth/papers/Nonsimultaneous%20Chains%20AJT%202011.pdf)

### Inferences
- Fairness options for the matcher:
  - Lexicographic hierarchy, as in the UK: first maximise the number of transplants, then the number of highly sensitised patients transplanted, and so on.
  - Weighted priority for cPRA, O blood group, waiting time and paediatric recipients.
  - Including compatible pairs, which bring in O donors.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve formal fairness-in-KPD papers (for example Dickerson et al. "price of fairness", or Farnadi et al.). They are not cited here.

## 7. Alternatives: ABO-incompatible (ABOi) and HLA-incompatible (HLAi) desensitisation

### Takeaway
Desensitisation is a valid fallback and beats staying on the waitlist. But ABOi grafts do slightly worse than compatible ones, and infection-related deaths are higher. KPD gives a compatible transplant when a match exists, which is why programmes try swaps first.

### Cited Findings
- HLAi desensitisation (Orandi et al., NEJM, 10 March 2016; 22 centres, n=1,025): 8-year patient survival was 76.5% for HLAi living-donor recipients. Comparators were 62.9% for "waitlist or deceased-donor transplant" and 43.9% for "waitlist only". The benefit held at all levels of donor-specific antibody positivity. — [NIDDK news](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/news/archive/2016/promising-result-reported-multicenter-kidney-transplantation-study); [Penn PEBLR summary](https://pennmedicine.org/departments-and-centers/department-of-surgery/education-and-training/residencies/general-surgery-residency/didactics/penn-evidence-based-literature-review/july-2016)
- ABOi meta-analysis (de Weerd & Betjes; 26 studies, 1,346 ABOi and 4,943 ABO-compatible patients): 1-year graft survival was 95.8% for ABOi, slightly worse than compatible (RR 0.97). 49% of ABOi deaths were infectious, compared with 13% in ABO-compatible recipients. — [repub.eur.nl](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/109799); see also [Scurt et al. meta-analysis, PubMed 31006573](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31006573/)
- In STEP, 53% of transplants were done across ABO incompatibility (exchange combined with ABOi), compared with 20% outside STEP. Programmes increasingly combine swaps and desensitisation. — [STEP 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10581625)
- The Czech KEP enables ABOi and HLAi transplants after desensitisation within exchanges. — [Biró 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6613834/)

### Inferences
- A matcher can treat "ABOi-with-low-titre" as a lower-weight or higher-cost edge rather than as absent. This mirrors STEP and Czech practice.

### Gaps
- I found no recent primary costing comparing desensitisation with KPD. Desensitisation is widely described as costly (IVIG, plasmapheresis, rituximab) but I have no verified dollar figures.

## 8. Optimisation methods used in practice

### Takeaway
The underlying problem is NP-hard once 3-cycles are allowed. With only 2-cycles it reduces to maximum-weight matching, solvable in polynomial time. Practice uses integer programming: the cycle formulation, edge and position-indexed formulations (PIEF/PICEF), and branch-and-price. These solve real national instances (UNOS, UK) quickly.

### Cited Findings
- PIEF/PICEF (Dickerson, Manlove, Plaut, Sandholm & Trimble, EC 2016): polynomial-size models with a tight LP relaxation. On real UNOS and UK NLDKSS data and large generated instances, they were competitive with all existing solvers and often orders of magnitude faster. — [arXiv 1606.01623](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.01623); [Glasgow eprints](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/119356)
- Barkel, Colley, Delorme, Manlove & Pettersson (2025) survey and benchmark OR approaches: 49 methods on 4,320 instances, more than 200,000 runs. Code, instances and a visualiser are public. — [arXiv 2504.04091](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04091)
- KidneyExchange.jl is an open-source Julia branch-and-price solver for the kidney exchange problem. — [HAL](https://hal-ujm.archives-ouvertes.fr/IRMAR-INSA/hal-03830810v3)
- Segev et al. 2005 used maximum edge-weight matching (2-way) for a national pool. — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2005/06/050608054631.htm)
- Pool sizes per run: UK about 250-300 pairs; Spain about 110; STEP about 22 (max 31 plus 1 altruistic donor); OPTN about 249 candidates added a year. — sources above

### Inferences
- For a hackathon at the pool sizes above, a cycle-formulation ILP using an open solver (OR-Tools/CBC/HiGHS) should solve in seconds. Use cycles of 3 or fewer and chains of 4 or fewer. A 2-cycle-only baseline can use networkx `max_weight_matching` as a comparison point.
- Background, not re-verified: Abraham, Blum & Sandholm (EC 2007) solved instances up to about 10,000 pairs with branch-and-price for cycles of 3 or fewer.
